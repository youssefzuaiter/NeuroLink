NeuroLink: A Privacy-Preserving Multi-Stream Behavioral Baseline Engine for Personal Typing Performance Monitoring

Differentially private N-of-1 autoencoder for longitudinal behavioral anomaly detection

Author Youssef Zuaiter Dataset 50,321 keybr.com lessons Period Dec 2024 – Jul 2026 Code github.com/youssefzuaiter/neurolink
Abstract

We present NeuroLink V4, a differentially private N-of-1 autoencoder that learns a personal behavioral baseline from typing history and detects longitudinal drift across three independent streams — Speed, Accuracy, and Motor control. Unlike population-based anomaly detectors, the system trains exclusively on a single user's data, requiring no external labels or cross-user comparisons. A shared encoder (15→32→16→10, Tanh) feeds three stream-specific decoder heads, each trained with Huber loss normalized by head size. Differential privacy is enforced via DP-SGD (Opacus, ε=9.997, δ=1×10⁻⁵). On a 50,321-lesson dataset spanning 492 days, the model achieves validation R²=0.9751 at a privacy cost of ΔR²=+0.0478 relative to a non-private baseline. Anomaly days show a mean WPM gap of −45.9 (p<0.001). The long-term research direction is passive neurological health monitoring through everyday typing behavior.

Section 1

Feature Engineering

Fifteen session-level features are derived from raw typing logs and organized into three semantically coherent streams. Features were selected through ablation — three candidates (SSF, day_of_week, ESRP) were dropped after per-feature R² analysis showed they contributed noise rather than signal, with the Motor stream R² improving from 0.666 to 0.980 upon their removal.

Section 2

Model Architecture & Training

The model is a multi-head autoencoder. A shared encoder compresses the 15-feature input into a 10-dimensional latent representation. Three stream-specific decoder heads independently reconstruct their respective feature subsets. This forces the latent space to capture cross-stream correlations while allowing each decoder to specialize.

Encoder: Linear(15→32) → Tanh → Linear(32→16) → Tanh → Linear(16→10) → Tanh. Tanh activations are used throughout to preserve signed deviations, which are critical for distinguishing above-baseline from below-baseline sessions.

Decoder heads: Each head follows the same pattern — Linear(10→16) → Tanh → Linear(16→5). All three share the same latent vector.

Loss function: Huber loss (δ=1.0) normalized by head size, summed across streams. Normalization ensures equal gradient contribution regardless of head size. Per-feature weights emphasize high-information features within each stream.

Training: DP-SGD via Opacus. EPOCHS=120, BATCH_SIZE=64, LR_MAX=2×10⁻³ (cosine schedule), MAX_GRAD_NORM=1.0, PATIENCE=20. Privacy budget tracked by RDP accountant; achieved ε=9.997 at δ=1×10⁻⁵.

Data split: Chronological 75/25 — first 75% of days for training, remaining 25% held out. No shuffle; temporal ordering preserved to prevent leakage.

Section 3

Results

The model was evaluated against four standard anomaly detection methods on the same dataset and split: Statistical Process Control (rolling mean ±2σ), Isolation Forest (contamination=0.10), One-Class SVM (nu=0.10, RBF kernel), and a non-DP autoencoder with identical architecture trained without Opacus.

Three evaluation metrics were used: anomaly rate (selectivity), WPM gap (whether flagged days are genuinely worse, tested via Welch t-test), and Dec 2024 detection rate (sensitivity on a known ground-truth anomaly period when average WPM dropped to approximately 60).

Section 4

Research Direction

The long-term hypothesis underlying this work is that typing behavioral drift is a passive, early signal for neurological change. Clinical literature establishes that fine motor degradation precedes visible symptoms in several conditions by months to years. Everyday typing is fine motor behavior, governed by the same neural pathways.

A system that monitors a personal behavioral baseline continuously — privately, on-device, without requiring any change in behavior — could potentially surface anomaly patterns worth discussing with a physician before traditional screening would identify them.

Important disclaimer. NeuroLink is a research project and is not a medical device. Nothing in this system constitutes medical advice or clinical diagnosis. If you have concerns about your neurological health, please consult a qualified physician.

Conditions for which the literature documents fine motor precursors include:

  • Early Parkinson's disease — resting tremor and rigidity detectable in motor timing years before clinical diagnosis
  • Multiple sclerosis — corticospinal tract disruption manifests as typing rhythm irregularities
  • Essential tremor — fine motor inconsistency at the character level
  • Cognitive fatigue — error entropy and speed variance shift measurably with mental load
  • Acute health changes — sleep deprivation, fever, and illness leave quantifiable motor traces
Section 5

How to Run It on Your Own Data

The system requires keybr.com typing data. The notebook runs in Google Colab with no local installation. Your raw data never leaves your machine — only the derived feature CSV is uploaded to Colab, and the trained model weights are discarded after the session.

1
Export your keybr data

Go to your keybr.com profile → Export. Download typing-data.json. This file contains your complete lesson history.

2
Open the Colab notebook

Upload typing-data.json and run all cells. The notebook generates the feature CSV, trains your personal model on a free T4 GPU (approximately 10 minutes), and produces a full report.

3
Read your behavioral report

The output includes per-stream LDI scores, anomaly timeline across your full history, comparison against four standard baselines, and a 90-day WPM forecast specific to your data.

Section 6

Limitations

N-of-1 design. All results are specific to one user. Generalizability across users is untested. Multi-user validation is a planned next step.

Absence of clinical ground truth. December 2024 is used as a proxy anomaly period based on observed WPM drop, not a medically confirmed event. No clinical labels are available.

Privacy budget. ε=10 is a moderate guarantee. Stronger privacy would require a larger dataset or architectural changes such as a larger batch size or fewer epochs.

Forecasting assumptions. The 90-day WPM projection assumes continued log-growth. Plateau effects at high WPM are not modeled.

Run it on your own data

Your model. Your baseline. Nothing shared. Works on any keybr.com history — the more lessons you have, the stronger the signal.
