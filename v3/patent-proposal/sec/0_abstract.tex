\begin{abstract}
We present NeuroLink 3.0, a privacy-preserving N-of-1 deep autoencoder for
detecting longitudinal behavioral drift in keystroke dynamics.
Unlike population-based approaches, our system learns a personalized baseline
from an individual's own typing history and flags sessions that deviate from
it using reconstruction error.
Trained on 42,501 keybr.com typing lessons spanning 17 months,
our undercomplete autoencoder (12\(\rightarrow\)32\(\rightarrow\)16\(\rightarrow\)
\textbf{10}\(\rightarrow\)16\(\rightarrow\)32\(\rightarrow\)12, 2,230 parameters)
achieves a variance-weighted validation \(R^2\) of \textbf{0.9586} under
differential privacy (\(\varepsilon{=}7.9983\), \(\delta{=}10^{-5}\)) via DP-SGD.
We introduce \emph{residual} Execution Velocity (EV) and Error-Speed Recovery
Penalty (ESRP) features — temporal deviation signals that resolve a structural
reconstruction ceiling present in rolling-mean formulations, raising EV \(R^2\)
from 0.234 to 0.954 and ESRP \(R^2\) from 0.113 to 0.854.
A bootstrap confidence interval derived from 2,000 resamples of the training
Longitudinal Drift Index (LDI) enables three-tier anomaly alerting
(HIGH\,/\,MARGINAL\,/\,NORMAL), and an empirically calibrated Ghost V3
predictor derives its drift-adjustment scale factor directly from data
rather than relying on a hand-tuned constant.
\end{abstract}
